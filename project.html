The Enterprise Dilemma: The Bullwhip Effect

In fast-moving logistics and distributor networks, disconnected systems create blind spots. Operations managers often rely on static monthly spreadsheets and intuition to forecast purchase orders. When unexpected demand surges or carrier delays strike, companies swing between two costly extremes: crippling warehouse stockouts that lose customers, or excess holding inventory that burns liquidity.

For our ISDS 4125 Capstone project under Professor Piccoli, our team set out to engineer a scalable, production-ready solution: SupplyPulse. My mission as team lead and lead systems architect was to build an automated data telemetry and predictive demand modeling engine that delivers proactive purchase recommendations before stockouts occur.

End-to-End System Architecture

SupplyPulse was architected using modular, enterprise-grade engineering principles. The system connects ingestion pipelines directly to statistical machine learning algorithms and exposes real-time data through a lightweight API.

Pipeline Blueprint

PHASE 01
Ingestion & ETL

Python automated jobs extract shipment and POS transaction records, transforming and loading clean rows into PostgreSQL.

PHASE 02
Analytical Engine

ARIMA & exponential smoothing models process seasonality, lead time variations, and 90% confidence bands.

PHASE 03
RESTful API

FastAPI endpoints serve forecasts, supplier risk indices, and inventory levels in millisecond response times.

PHASE 04
Interactive UI

Responsive executive dashboard presenting charts, telemetry tables, and automated replenishment alerts.

Key Capabilities & Innovation Features

  • Dynamic 6-Month Demand Horizon: Instead of static linear projections, SupplyPulse incorporates multi-variable time series with visual confidence intervals (90%), allowing supply planners to simulate best-case, expected, and worst-case inventory burn rates.
  • Algorithmic Safety Stock & Reorder Points: Computes dynamic safety stock levels accounting for fluctuating supplier transit lead times, mitigating sudden logistics shocks.
  • Live Shipment Telemetry & Risk Scoring: Evaluates incoming freight routes, flagging carrier delays (e.g. ETA overruns) and quantifying carrier on-time reliability scores.
  • Role-Based Executive Summary Cards: Provides C-suite and warehouse supervisors instant visibility into total units held, average turnover rates, and fill rate benchmarks (maintaining 97.4%).

Technical Challenges & What I Learned

Overcoming Data Noise & Outliers: Real-world supply data contains irregular gaps and seasonal spikes (promotions, weather disruptions). I implemented anomaly detection filtering using interquartile range (IQR) thresholds in Pandas to prevent model overfitting.

Bridging Analytics with Executive Usability: One of my proudest breakthroughs was designing the user interface. Non-technical logistics managers do not want raw math; they need clear, actionable decisions. I structured the dashboard so critical alerts ("Order ETA Delayed" or "Reorder Point Breached") are immediately noticeable with intuitive visual hierarchies.

Collaborative Leadership: Serving as project lead reinforced the importance of clear Scrum milestones, consistent API contracts between front-end and back-end team members, and proactive client communication.
